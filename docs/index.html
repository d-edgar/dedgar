ChatHarbor

A place to chat, compare answers, and work through ideas.

I built a native Mac app for comparing model responses and working through ideas without jumping between separate tools.

macOS · SwiftUI · SwiftData · Open source

Explore ChatHarbor → View source →

Why I built it this way

I wanted ChatHarbor to feel at home on a Mac and give people a choice of cloud or local models. Behind the scenes, one shared interface connects them, so switching models doesn’t mean learning a different app.

Under the hood: Swift

Curious about the code? This is the interface the model providers share.

func chat(
    model: String,
    messages: [ChatMessage],
    parameters: ChatParameters,
    onToken: @escaping (String) -> Void
) async throws -> ChatResult

A little about me

Follow along on Instagram ↗

Helping people has always been the best part of IT.

I’ve worked in IT since 2006, when I began my degree at Old Dominion University. What started as a computer hobby became a career helping people use technology and keeping their computers running.

I’m the Manager of Technical Support Services at Christopher Newport University. I lead support teams and manage Apple devices with Jamf Pro, software that helps IT teams set up and maintain Macs.

Outside of work, I run, build apps, and share what I’ve learned with the Mac admin community at conferences like ACCS.

Sharing what works

In my ACCS 2026 talk, I walked through using Jamf App Catalog and App Installers to handle app packaging, versioning, and updates. The aim: less manual installer maintenance, with a setup other administrators can follow.

If you’re working through something similar, the slides and resources are here to help.
